# Regional climate predictions

Predictions come from the **Mars Climate Database 6.2**, climatology average-solar scenario, credited **LMD/OU/IAA/ESA/CNES**. [Official model interface and credits](https://www-mars.lmd.jussieu.fr/mcd_python/). This is a general-circulation-model climatology, not measured weather or an operational current-event forecast.

The bundled regional grids were queried at the USGS crater centers (Jezero 18.41°N, 77.69°E; Gale 5.44°S, 137.70°E), 1.5 m above the modeled surface, high-resolution topography option enabled. Data cover solar longitude every 15° and local true solar time every hour. `assets/data/climate_model.json` retains each query URL, exported ASCII URL and SHA-256. Original credited numeric exports live in `assets/data/climate/`. `tools/fetch_climate.py` reproduces the downloads; it never generates missing values.

The application uses periodic bilinear interpolation of the 24 unique seasonal nodes and 24 unique hourly nodes. The raw redundant Ls=360° / LT=24 h endpoints remain in the exports for auditing; interpolation wraps to the first unique node. Values are regional center-model estimates. A selected pin supplies local true solar time, not invented terrain or subpixel weather.

Model variables: air temperature, surface pressure, eastward/northward wind, surface temperature, air density, daily dust optical depth, incident surface solar flux, dust deposition, thermal inertia, albedo, water-vapor column, water-vapor volume mixing ratio, CO₂ volume mixing ratio, and pressure scale height. Wind speed and meteorological wind-from azimuth derive from the two wind components. Sampled daily extrema use the hourly temperature grid. Temperature rate is a centered hourly difference converted to Earth hours. Water column converts kg/m² to precipitable µm; dust deposition converts kg/m²/s to mg/cm²/sol. Pressure scale height converts m to km; CO₂ displays explicitly as percent CO₂ by volume, not a claim about all atmospheric species.

Relative humidity over ice uses model water-vapor VMR and near-surface pressure, with ice saturation vapor pressure from Murphy & Koop (2005), equation 7, [DOI 10.1256/qj.04.94](https://doi.org/10.1256/qj.04.94). The approximation uses model scale height for the 1.5 m pressure adjustment. Supersaturation is retained rather than silently clipped; predictions are not validated against current rover measurements.

Every modeled display is labeled **Predicted**. Historical NASA station observations remain separate and retain their source dates. Unquantified model uncertainty, seasonal interpolation, dust scenario, unresolved terrain and regional spatial resolution limit these predictions. Current storms, ionizing radiation, caves, subsurface ice depth, soil perchlorates and relay blackouts are not inferred from weather patterns; unsupported fields say **Not modeled**. No prediction establishes route safety.
